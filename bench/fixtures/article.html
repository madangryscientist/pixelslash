Why pixel diffs lie to you

Posted 12 March · 4 min read

A screenshot is the output of a long pipeline: layout, font rasterisation, compositing, colour management. Two runs of the same page can disagree in places nobody can see, because each stage makes small choices that depend on the machine it ran on.

The signal is the change a person would notice; the rest is weather.

Teams end up reviewing hundreds of flagged shots a week, and learn to approve without looking. That is the moment a visual test stops testing anything.

  • Pin the browser and the fonts.
  • Freeze animations and the clock.
  • Compare pixels, then localise the change.

None of this is exotic, but it has to be done every time, by default, or the noise comes back.
